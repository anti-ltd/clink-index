Animated backgrounds on any theme, the standard iPhone Arabic layout, words that sync between your devices, and a much smarter autocorrect for words you typed one key off.

## New

- **Animated backgrounds work on any theme now.** Rain, embers, starfield and the rest used to belong to whichever theme carried them, so wanting one on Nord meant copying Nord and editing it. Theme → Animated background now picks the look on its own, and it takes its colours from whatever theme you're on, so it suits all of them. Leave it on Theme to keep doing what the theme says. Speed, density, size, brightness and the press reaction are all there too.
- **The search bar takes questions.** Type "how do i add a language" or "where do i make the keys bigger" into the bar on any tab and Apple Intelligence works out which setting or FAQ answer you mean, on the phone. Its picks sit above the ordinary word matches with a ✨ on them, and every one is a real page, it points, it doesn't write. Needs Apple Intelligence; the plain search is untouched, and there's a switch for it under Integrations → Apple Intelligence.
- **The standard iPhone Arabic layout.** Layouts → Arabic now offers both arrangements Apple does. **Arabic** is the one the system keyboard uses, eleven keys across the top two rows, with ة, ء and ى on caps of their own. **Arabic (PC)** is the arrangement Clink already had, matching a desktop Arabic keyboard. Anyone already typing in Arabic stays on the keys they know; the new one is a tap away.
- **Arabic-Indic numerals, if you want them.** Languages → Numerals puts ١٢٣ on the Arabic keyboard's 123 page. Off by default, since most Arabic typing uses 123. The number row above the letters stays Western either way, so with both on you have ١٢٣ and 123 on screen at once, and holding any digit still gives you the other set.
- **Four custom key-click sound packs.** Pick Tactile Brown, Clicky Blue, Typewriter, or Marble under Feel → Sound. Each pack rotates a few short clicks so typing doesn't sound like one loop. Needs Full Access; System Click still works without it.
- **Your words can travel to your other devices.** General → iCloud Sync → Learned words carries your custom dictionary and the words Clink has picked up from you to your iPad and back. It's off until you turn it on, and it's encrypted before it leaves, under a key that only exists in your own iCloud Keychain. Words are **merged**, never replaced: what you taught your phone this morning and what you taught your iPad last week both survive, in either direction, which also means a word you delete on one device stays deleted, rather than coming back the next time the other one syncs.
- **Clipboard history can skip images.** Copying a picture used to fill a slot in your history with it. Clipboard → Paste → Ignore images keeps images out and saves copied text as usual, and it's on to start with, turn it off if you want pictures kept too.

## Improved

- **An animated background no longer needs the background switched on.** It used to require "Show background", which meant the drifting light only ever played over a solid slab or a photo. Leave the background off and the particles drift over the app you're typing in, with nothing behind the keys but that app.

## Fixed

- **Recent emoji stop vanishing.** Tap a few emoji, close the keyboard, and the Recent tab would be there the next time you opened it, then gone the time after. Emoji are saved by the keyboard, but everything else in Clink is saved by the app, and both wrote the whole settings file at once: whichever one saved next while holding an older copy of the file put the empty list back. The recents on file are now the ones that count, and only tapping an emoji (or Clear recent emoji) can change them, so nothing else can undo your last tap, no matter which app you were typing in.
- **Arabic autocorrect stops "fixing" words you meant.** Two causes. The Arabic dictionary was built from subtitles and written sentences, nobody texts like that, so everyday spoken words were missing outright (كيفك، شلونك، ازيك، كتير، خلص) or ranked so rare they read as typos (بس، ليش، بدي، عشان، يلا). Around sixty of them are now in the dictionary at the frequency people actually use them. Separately, a two-letter word the keyboard already knew could still be swapped for a commoner lookalike a single key away, حل became كل. Two letters carry no shape to judge by, so that now only ever appears as a suggestion you can tap, never a silent change.
- **The Arabic keyboard types Arabic punctuation.** The number and symbol pages were handing out the Latin `?` `,` `;` `%`, which curve the wrong way in right-to-left text. They're now ؟ ، ؛ ٪, with the Latin ones a hold away for links and mixed-script writing. A question ending in ؟ also counts as a finished sentence now, so what you type next is predicted properly.
- **"I'd", "he'd", "she'd" and "we'd" get their apostrophes.** Every other contraction was on the list the keyboard fixes, "isn't", "aren't", "don't", "you're", but the four 'd forms never were, so "guess id call you later" stayed exactly as typed. They're in now, with "ain't", "o'clock", "how'd", "why'd", "when's", "who've" and the "everyone's" family.
- **Words like "id" and "shed" are now judged by the sentence, not by a list.** These collide with real words, so the keyboard has to decide each time whether you meant "I'd" or an ID. It used to decide from a hand-written list of lead-in words, which only ever covered what someone thought to write down, everything else defaulted to leaving your text alone. It now reads the answer from the dictionary's own word-pair data: "your id" stays an ID, "and id" becomes "and I'd", "the shed" stays a shed, "said shed call" becomes "said she'd call". The word after counts too, so "id like" turns into "I'd like" at the start of a message where there's nothing in front to go on, and the word before that can overrule it, which is how "a shell like that" stays a shell. Where the dictionary has nothing to say about a pairing the old list still decides, so nothing that worked before stopped working.
- **Finishing a word by swiping the next one no longer skips the fix.** Space completes the word you just typed, apostrophe, spelling, text replacement and all, but starting a swipe didn't, so the word before a glide kept whatever you'd tapped out. Type "isnt", swipe the next word, and it stayed "isnt". A glide now finishes the word in front of it the same way a space does.
- **A number fat-fingered into a word no longer ruins it.** A digit ended a word as far as Clink was concerned, so "hello5" reached the corrector as nothing at all and "hel8lo" reached it as "lo", which it then helpfully corrected on its own. Words are now read straight through a digit, and a stray one is undone either way it happens: typed on top of the letter beneath it ("hell9" → "hello") or as an extra tap ("peopl4e" → "people"). Things you meant to type with a number in them, mp3, covid19, iphone15, b2b, are still left exactly alone.
- **Typing a whole word one key off now gets fixed.** Rest your hand a key to the left and "hello" comes out "gwkki", every letter wrong, all in the same direction. Clink was charging that as five separate mistakes, which made it score worse than a word with one random slip, so it never got corrected. It now tests the obvious explanation first: the hand was off by one. Only for words the dictionary doesn't recognise, and only when every letter shifts the same way.
- **Words begun one letter late are a tap away.** Miss the "t" in "there" and you're left with "here", an ordinary word, so nothing offered you anything else. The bar now keeps a slot for the word you get by putting a letter back on the front. It's only ever a suggestion; "here" stays "here" unless you tap.
- **A word learned in one language stops interfering with another.** Everything Clink learned went into one pile, so a French word you'd typed twice counted as correctly spelled while you were writing English, and a word Clink treats as correct is one it won't fix, which is how a two-letter slip in your English survived because it happened to be a word in your French. Words are now remembered per language: they defend your spelling in the language you typed them in, and only nudge the ranking elsewhere, since a name is a name whichever language you're in. Words you added yourself still apply everywhere, and words learned before this update are judged by their script, so nothing Arabic can ever affect your English again. The Learned words list shows which language each one belongs to.
- **Typing a word and deleting it no longer teaches it.** Clink learns a word the moment you finish it, which meant a typo you backspaced away a second later still counted, do it twice and the keyboard started defending the mistake. It now watches what you keep: anything you delete right after typing is taken back out of the learned words, along with the word-pairing it created.
