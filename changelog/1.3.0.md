Clink 1.3 is the enormous one: neural typing in twelve languages, on-device AI writing tools, programmable actions and panels, Korean and Vietnamese input, animated themes, 3D and metal keys, pets, a vastly more powerful custom-key system, smarter clipboard tools, and a ground-up settings redesign — all while making the keyboard faster, calmer, and much harder to confuse.

## New

- **Neural typing in twelve languages.** Clink's on-device Core ML engine now powers prediction and autocorrect in Arabic, Bulgarian, Croatian, English, French, German, Hungarian, Italian, Malay, Portuguese, Russian, and Spanish. It reads the full sentence, catches real-word mistakes that ordinary spell-checking misses, understands modern and unfamiliar words through subword tokenization, and uses a spatial model to tell a likely fat-finger slip from a word you meant to keep. Everything runs privately on the Neural Engine.
- **A complete on-device AI writing toolbox.** Open AI Tools from the keyboard to proofread, rewrite, shorten, change tone, translate, or draft a reply with Apple Intelligence. Build your own actions and edit every prompt, then see exactly what changed with underlines, an inline diff, or a clean change list. Results can replace the selection, insert beside it, or go to the clipboard — the visual markup never leaks into the text.
- **Program your own keyboard in Python.** Custom Actions can transform what you type, while Custom Panels can become calculators, snippet boards, pickers, and entirely new keyboard UIs. Both have a built-in editor and live run console, can be shared as files, and execute locally in a small on-device sandbox. They're available to everyone in 1.3.
- **Korean, Vietnamese, Croatian, Arabic, Bulgarian, and Hungarian support.** Korean gets a full dubeolsik Hangul compositor; Vietnamese supports Telex, VNI, and VIQR; Croatian gets its proper QWERTZ layout and dictionary; Arabic adds a native layout, tashkeel key, diacritics, Arabic-Indic number alternates, and more held forms; Bulgarian gets both Phonetic and БДС layouts; and Hungarian gains its own dictionary and a fully localized app. Clink now chooses the right layout when you add a language, too.
- **Custom keys grew into a layout toolkit.** Give a key separate tap, shift, hold, and directional-swipe actions; put custom keys on the number row; add persistent keys beside the space bar; create true spacers; choose caps from a visual character and SF Symbol picker; and jump straight into any built-in or custom panel. A single-character key can even mix panel shortcuts into its Gboard-style long-press alternates. Whole layouts can now be imported, exported, duplicated, and shared.
- **Animated, reactive theme backgrounds.** Thirteen GPU-drawn effects — including Aurora, Rain, Embers, Starfield, Snow, Confetti, Fireworks, and Lava — drift behind the keys and react as you type. Tune their colours, speed, density, brightness, blend, and reaction strength; Clink automatically pauses them for Low Power Mode, heat, memory pressure, and Reduce Motion.
- **A huge new theme and key-style library.** The catalog nearly doubled with new colour families, typography showcases, classics, Liquid Glass tints, brushed-metal finishes, and raised 3D keycaps. Themes also gain key outlines, more press and entrance effects, per-letter animations, delete animations, properly rendered named typefaces, and a refreshed app icon.
- **Clink Pets.** Seventeen little friends can live on the keyboard, watch your taps, celebrate finished words, react to deletes, nap when you stop, and wake up when you return. Every species has its own personality and movement, and you can choose its corner, size, and how lively it should be.
- **A smarter clipboard.** Organize clips into named groups, filter links, email addresses, phone numbers, and images with automatic badges, and send AI results straight to the clipboard manager. URL detection now knows the difference between an actual link and an innocent word like "Cute."
- **More control over text and feel.** Text replacements work directly in Clink, smart quotes and punctuation spacing each have their own switch, accent and symbol bars can be saved per layout, and a new 2D haptic feel pad lets you choose sharpness and intensity together with a live preview under your finger.
- **Neural autocorrect for eleven more languages.** The Core ML autocorrect model now ships for Arabic, Bulgarian, Croatian, French, German, Hungarian, Italian, Malay, Portuguese, Russian, and Spanish, on top of English. Each gets the ✦ Neural capsule on the Languages page, and the full neural correction path (contextual real-word fixes, LM-aware ranking) instead of bigram-only autocorrect.
- **See what the AI changed.** The AI Tools result panel now marks up the model's edits so you can tell at a glance what it touched, and the header shows a live count ("3 changes"). Pick the style on the AI Tools page — **Underline** (default; added words underlined, result stays readable), **Inline diff** (struck-out originals sit next to their replacements), **Change list** (a clean result with every before → after edit listed below), or **Off** — and a live sample right under the picker shows exactly how each looks. The choice applies to every action, built-in or your own. Actions that write fresh text (replies, drafts, translations) are never marked, and Replace / Insert / Copy always apply the clean output, never the marks.
- **Haptic feel pad.** The Feel › Haptics page replaces the Style dropdown and Strength slider with a single 2D pad — drag anywhere to set both at once. The X axis is sharpness (Soft → Rigid, with the five named styles as gridlines you still snap onto), the Y axis is intensity. A live preview haptic fires as you drag (throttled) so you audition the exact feel with every nudge, and the Haptics tab's preview is now a single Try button instead of the full keyboard.
- **Neural-autocorrect badges.** The Languages page now marks every language that ships the Core ML autocorrect model with a ✦ Neural capsule, and shows a one-line legend when any language in view has it. Today that's English; more land as each model is trained. No badge means bigram-only autocorrect.
- **Korean Hangul input.** A full dubeolsik (두벌식) jamo compositor breaks each syllable you type into onset, nucleus, and coda — then combines them into the right block. Supports all 11,172 modern Hangul syllables plus most of the ¹⁄₁₅ archaic ones the standard allows. Works with any keyboard layout; swap your language key to Korean and start typing.
- **Vietnamese Telex/VNI/VIQR input.** Type your Vietnamese diacritics the way you already do — Telex, VNI, or VIQR — and the compositor places the tone mark and letter modifiers on the right character, exactly like the system keyboard.
- **Key outline preference.** Pick an outline style for your keys under Theme → Shape: none, thin, subtle, or prominent. The outline follows your theme's key colour (or a separate tint you choose) and scales with the gap between keys so it stays crisp on any style.
- **Neural engine: triple ANE warmup.** `prewarm()` now runs 3 throwaway inferences on load instead of 1, ensuring the Neural Engine is fully awake before the first keystroke instead of paying cold-start latency mid-type.
- **Neural LM: contextual real-word correction.** A new correction path uses the LM's full-sentence understanding to catch real-word typos that existing paths miss — valid dictionary words that don't fit the context, like "buck" → "book" in "that was a good win in my buck". Fires when the literal's LM probability falls below a threshold and an edit-2 neighbour scores at least 100× higher in context. Works on both the per-keystroke bar pass (heavy tick) and the space-commit decision.
- **Program your own keyboard, in Python.** Custom Actions and Custom Panels are out of beta and available to everyone — no longer behind a hidden flag. Find them in the app under Customization. **Custom Actions** are little scripts that transform text as you type (UPPERCASE the current word, title-case a sentence, drop in a shrug); write one in Python, and it appears behind the keyboard's action button. **Custom Panels** are whole custom keyboard UIs you build in Python — a calculator, a snippet board, a picker — that live in the panel switcher next to Clipboard and Notepad. Every action and panel is yours to edit in a built-in code editor with a live run console, and shares as a file so you can send your creations to a friend. Scripts run in a small sandboxed interpreter on-device, so nothing you type ever leaves your phone.

## Improved

- **The whole app now feels native to iOS.** Settings were rebuilt from top to bottom with system navigation, Liquid Glass controls, frosted cards, segmented tabs, native sheets, and swipeable pages. Custom Actions and Panels use the same card surfaces, inline toggles, and direct drag handles as the rest of Clink.
- **Typing is dramatically faster under load.** Autocorrect and prediction moved off the touch path; duplicate work, main-thread spell checks, huge edit-neighbour searches, excess allocations, settings-write storms, and avoidable Neural Engine passes were removed. Haptics now land the instant a key goes down, while background language work yields to touches and rendering so fast typing stays fluid.
- **Autocorrect is more accurate and more respectful.** Clink now uses its own bundled dictionaries consistently instead of depending on whichever iOS spell-checkers happen to be installed. Ranking combines context, word frequency, edit distance, and keyboard geometry without letting context bully a word you clearly typed; names, slang, greetings, chat words, custom words, and words from another active language are far less likely to be "fixed" into nonsense.
- **Apple Intelligence is one simple switch.** The Apple Intelligence control under Integrations is now the single master switch for AI completions, correction help, translation, and AI Tools. On supported hardware it is ready out of the box, with per-feature controls still available on the AI page.
- **Layouts and modes work together more naturally.** Split keyboard can now run inside one-handed mode, language keys can show a real flag, globe, or region code, and space/shift/delete use familiar system proportions while custom layouts retain their own sizing controls.
- **Apple Intelligence is now one switch.** The separate "Enable AI" toggle is gone; the single **Apple Intelligence** switch on Integrations is the only master control. On capable hardware the AI assists (completions, autocorrect help, translation, the AI Tools panel) are on out of the box instead of waiting for a second switch. The AI tab now shows only the per-feature assists and the availability readout.
- **Spell-checking runs entirely on Clink's own dictionaries.** Autocorrect, suggestions, and swipe vocabulary no longer consult the iOS system spell-checker — everything comes from Clink's bundled language lexicons. Corrections now behave identically on every device regardless of which OS dictionaries are installed, and the ~250ms cold spell-checker warm-up on first appearance is gone. The Languages list shows exactly the languages Clink ships a dictionary for.
- **Smarter, leaner neural engine.** The neural language model now uses subword (BPE) tokenization, so out-of-vocabulary words like "haptics" or "rizz" carry a real context signal instead of collapsing to a single unknown token, sharpening prediction and correction around modern and uncommon words. The bundled model weights are also roughly half the size (about 2.3 MB, down from 4.7 MB per language).
- **Faster, calmer typing under load.** The background correction and next-word passes now run at a lower scheduling priority so they yield to touch handling and rendering instead of competing with the frame that paints your key press. A duplicate suggestion refresh that fired on every keystroke is collapsed to one pass, spell candidates are built more cheaply, and correction ranking keeps the same results with less work. The net effect is fewer stutters during fast typing, with no change to what appears in the bar.
- **More accurate autocorrect ranking.** Context is now a bonus-only signal that can never penalise a correct fix, its influence is tuned so the language model informs ranking without overriding a word you clearly typed, and correction candidates are ranked by frequency discounted by edit distance — so a close-but-uncommon fix like "spaetial" → "spatial" survives instead of being buried under common but distant words.
- **Custom Actions and Panels use the app's cards.** Both management pages were rebuilt on the same `CardSection` surface as the rest of the app instead of a native inset-grouped list, so they read as one set. Reorder by dragging the handle on each row (the toolbar Edit button is gone), enable or disable with the inline toggle, and delete from the editor's Manage section.
- **Haptics fire the instant you press.** The key-press haptic now runs synchronously on the touch path instead of being deferred to the next runloop hop. On a warm engine the Core Haptics call is sub-millisecond, so the old deferral solved a non-problem while letting the haptic land up to ~57ms late during fast typing, behind the glass bloom and corrector work flooding the main queue. (Sound playback stays deferred — its `setActive`/`play` is genuinely slow; a haptic is not.)
- **Much faster spell-correction.** The edit-distance trie walk that powers corrections was the single heaviest path on the corrector (3–50ms for a distance-2 scan). The walk now tracks the row minimum inline during the DP fill instead of rescanning, reuses a single scratch row, and spells candidate words only after the sort and trim — so a scan that finds hundreds of terminals builds only the handful of strings it actually returns. Same results; the recurrence and visited set are unchanged.
- **Calmer settings writes.** Dragging the feel pad (or any rapid field burst) used to persist and post a cross-process reload on every tick — ~60/sec — storming a running keyboard with reloads that competed with touch delivery and delayed haptics. Writes are now coalesced into one save and one reload once the burst settles, with an immediate flush on backgrounding so a pending write can't be lost to suspension.
- **Split keyboard and one-handed mode compose.** The two layouts no longer disable each other — split runs inside the one-handed frame, so you can keep both on and flip between them. Form layout remains exclusive to both.
- **Real flags in Languages.** Language cells now render the same vector flag artwork the language-key cap uses, instead of the emoji flag character, so the page and the key read as one set.
- **Adjusting a control no longer flips the tab.** A slider or the feel pad mid-drag now suppresses the page's swipe-to-tab gesture, so editing a value can't slide the tab out from under you.
- **Refreshed app icon.** Updated the app icon (and the clear and dark variants).
- **Neural LM: one ANE pass per keystroke (was up to 3).** The LM distribution (`lmDist`) is now built once in the background corrector block and shared across the bar pass, the correction scorer, and the real-word typo gate. Previously each of `shortWordCorrection`, `realWordCorrection`, and `scoredSpellingCorrection` built its own distribution independently.
- **Neural LM: interpolated re-ranking.** Completion re-ranking in the bar now blends LM probability with lexicon frequency (`score = lmLogP + 0.3 * freqLogP`), so a rare word that happens to fit context can't leapfrog a far more common completion.
- **Neural LM: higher scorer context weight.** `contextWeight` rises from 0.15 to 0.35 when the neural LM is active — the LM's dense distribution is far more reliable than the sparse bigram, so context can meaningfully influence which correction candidate wins.
- **Neural LM: LM-aware real-word context gate.** The real-word typo fix (`realWordCorrection`) now uses the full left-context LM distribution for its hard collocation gate instead of the single-previous-word bigram, capturing multi-word dependencies the bigram misses.
- **Async haptic dispatch for lower touch-event latency.** The Core Haptics `player.start(atTime:)` call (a synchronous cross-process IPC to the haptic server) is now dispatched via `DispatchQueue.main.async`, so it never blocks the touch event. The haptic still lands within a frame — perceptually identical — while the touch path returns immediately, keeping fast typing fluid.
- **Removed the keep-warm haptic timer.** The 1.2s `Timer` that re-primed the Taptic Engine between keystrokes is gone. The engine's `isAutoShutdownEnabled` is already `false`, and the existing `stoppedHandler`/`resetHandler` restart it automatically on system interruption. This eliminates recurring main-thread wakeups during typing and scrolling with no change to haptic responsiveness.
- **Minimised press-state tracking allocations.** `RenderDriver.pressDown`/`release`/etc. now update `pressed` incrementally (O(1) insert/remove per key) instead of recomputing `heldCounts.keys ∪ lingering` as a new `Set` on every call. Eliminates two `Set` allocations per keystroke — ~8 allocations saved across the `pressDown`→`release`→`startLinger`→`cancelLinger` lifecycle.
- **More resilient correction cache.** The space-commit correction cache now keeps a tiny MRU of recent decisions instead of a single slot, including "no correction" results. Fast typing, host callbacks, or a quiet-gated checker pass are less likely to evict the exact fix the next space press needs, avoiding last-moment synchronous correction work.
- **Snappier metal and 3D keys.** The brushed-metal and 3D-keycap styles now draw their resting and pressed looks as two cached layers that cross-fade the instant you press, instead of rebuilding the whole gradient stack on every tap. The keys look the same but cost far less to animate, so they stay smooth even during fast typing.
- **Brand new app icon.** Clink now wears a glowing neon keyboard, indigo keys and spacebar lit up on a near-black tile. It matches the rest of our apps instead of standing out as the lavender one, and comes with proper dark and clear versions.
- **Real flags on the language key.** The language key now shows a full country flag filling the cap, with a soft rim and gloss, instead of the small emoji flag character.
- **Shinier metal keys.** The metal key material picked up a brighter reflection line and a soft diagonal sheen, so it reads more like polished metal than tinted plastic.
- Power management

Arabic brings the Arabic keys, Russian brings ЙЦУКЕН, French brings AZERTY, so a new language works right away instead of being stuck on QWERTY. The home and options buttons sit in the real nav bar, the Theme options button is a normal menu, and every popup (gradient editor, fine-tune editors, custom key editor, changelog, sources, app language) is a native sheet.

## Fixed

- **Fast typing no longer trips over the keyboard.** A long typo or a correction on space no longer freezes the UI; the suggestion bar no longer flickers or disagrees with the correction that will be committed; and spaces no longer become line breaks when a thumb lands high on the bar.
- **The keyboard stays readable and intact.** Suggestion text remains visible over transparent and dark host backgrounds, large emoji stay inside their cells, Liquid Glass sheds caches before dropping its full appearance, imported themes preview themselves, recent emoji survive updates, and named display fonts finally render on the keys.
- **Dozens of correctness and stability fixes.** Empty amount fields reliably open the number pad, AI writing actions preserve the input language unless you explicitly choose Translate, haptics survive audio interruptions, keyboard sounds stop spiking, Vietnamese tone removal works on accented vowels, and clipboard, notepad, popup, theme, and gesture edge cases have been cleaned up throughout.
- **Your own words stay put, especially across languages.** With two languages active (e.g. English + German) a word like "apps" no longer gets silently rewritten into a foreign-language lookalike ("paps") — correction candidates are scoped to the language the typed word belongs to. Modern keyboard vocabulary the subtitle corpus underweights ("apps", "dm", "tweet", "emoji", "wifi", "login" and many more) and common greetings ("hey", "hi", "yo", "bye") are protected, and single-letter transposition fixes now go through the scorer so a real word can't be blindly swapped for an equally- or less-common neighbour ("from" won't flip to "form").
- **Two-letter fixes read context better.** A short slip like "he ir" now corrects to "he is" using the previous word, rather than jumping to the spatially closer "or".
- **AI Tools no longer translate on their own.** Proofread, Rewrite, and the rest now keep your text in its original language — only Translate changes it. Previously an action like Proofread on Spanish text could hand back an English translation.
- **Number pad appears in empty amount fields.** Fields that request a number or phone pad but are focused while empty (like a payment "Enter Amount" box) sometimes came up as the letter keyboard, because the field type was sampled before the host connected. Clink now re-checks once it's live, so the digit pad shows.
- **Suggestion bar stays legible on any background.** On a transparent keyboard the bar text could vanish against a dark host app, since its colour is only guaranteed to contrast the key faces, not the backdrop it floats on. The bar now uses its own text colour that falls back to the key-fill colour whenever the normal text wouldn't read against the backdrop, so suggestions and panel icons stay visible everywhere.
- **Named typefaces render on the keys again.** A theme using a bundled display face (Futura, Marker Felt, Copperplate and the like) could still fall back to the system font because the app's font-design override re-resolved the custom glyph as San Francisco. The override is now skipped for named typefaces, so the chosen face renders. (Follow-on to the B70 fix.)
- **Named typefaces render on the keys again.** Themes using a bundled display face (Futura, Marker Felt, Copperplate and the like) were silently falling back to the system font on the keyboard itself, because the `Font(_ uiFont:)` bridge is lossy in the extension process and frequently drops the custom family for San Francisco. Named typefaces now resolve to their concrete PostScript name and go through `Font.custom`, which SwiftUI honours verbatim, so the chosen face actually renders. The canvas also resets an inherited `fontDesign` so app-chrome theming can't override the per-glyph font.
- **Haptics no longer crash after an audio interruption.** The Core Haptics stopped/reset handlers were asserting MainActor isolation off the main queue (Core Haptics dispatches them on its own queue, never the main queue), which trapped with `EXC_BREAKPOINT`. They now hop to the MainActor instead of assuming they're already there.
- **Large emoji no longer overlap in the picker.** Emoji that render wider than their cell — a high glyph-size setting, or naturally double-width sequences like families and couples — were spilling across their neighbours and stacking on top of them. They now scale down to fit their cell plus one gap, so every emoji stays in its own space.
- **Theme import previews the theme you're importing.** Opening a shared theme now shows that theme in the preview — in its own light or dark appearance — instead of rendering your currently selected one, so you can see exactly what you're about to add before you tap Import.
- **Named typefaces show up on the keys again.** Themes that use a bundled display face (Futura, Marker Felt, Copperplate and the like) were silently falling back to the system font because those families don't ship every weight. Keys now resolve the typeface by family so the chosen face actually renders.
- **Liquid Glass stays on longer.** The keyboard now clears hidden memory first — cached theme photos, clipboard thumbnails, AI sessions, neural-model state, decoded sounds and haptics, and stale suggestion work — before falling back to a flat theme. Liquid Glass only drops to the emergency solid render under critical memory pressure, so the keyboard keeps its full look much more often while still protecting against iOS ejecting the extension.
- **Korean emoji search combines syllables again.** Typing Hangul in emoji search showed separate jamo instead of normal syllable blocks, so searches for Korean words missed. Emoji search now uses the same syllable composition as the rest of the keyboard.
- **Space no longer types a line break.** Pressing space mid-word could hit the return key instead if your thumb landed on the upper part of the space bar, a side effect of the tap-routing that keeps fast typing from splitting words. High space presses now go to space or the letters above, and return only fires when you actually tap it.
- **Balloon key popups look right now.** The balloon used to be a flat colour slab with a kink where the bubble met the key, and on glass themes the key's own letter showed through its stem. The bubble now flows smoothly into the key, gets a lit-to-shaded colour ramp, hides the letter underneath, and flips its magnified letter to dark on light accent colours so it stays readable.
- **Stray line breaks while typing.** A word could drop to a new line on its own mid-sentence. Committed words now stay on one line, so only the return key starts a new one.
- **Smart clipboard URL tags.** Plain words like "Cute" no longer get a URL badge. Only clips that look like real links (with a domain dot) are tagged as URLs.
- **Engine card spacing.** The neural stats block had no room at the bottom of its card, and Reset stats is red now so it reads as a clear-everything action.
- **Suggestion bar stops flickering.** A word in the suggestion bar no longer pops up and then gets swapped for another as you keep typing. It showed most in German, where the spell checker offers a fan of compound words. The instant bar and the slower spell-check pass now order the bar the same way.
- **Recent emoji survive updates.** Your recently used emoji list no longer clears when you update the app, switch profiles, or change a setting in the app after typing with the keyboard. Recents stay on the device instead of being wiped by a stale save or an empty profile snapshot.
- **Clipboard top blur.** iOS 26's automatic scroll-edge blur frosted a hard band across the top of the clipboard and saved-notes panels, behind the toolbar and filter chips. That system effect is off now on every scrolling surface in those panels, including the filter-chips row.
- **Text replacements expand everywhere.** Shortcut expansions now commit on space independent of the autocorrect toggle, including search bars and fields with autocorrect off where they previously only showed in the bar.
- **Smoother feedback when typing fast.** Per-keypress haptic-engine lag is gone (warm-engine tracking), sound playback is off the touch path so it no longer drops keystrokes, and the "system click suddenly very loud until you switch apps" bug is fixed.
- **Row swipe vs tab swipe.** Swiping a row to reveal or delete in Clipboard and Notepad no longer also flips the tab.
- **Autocorrect leaves chat words alone.** Common slang and texting words like "lol", "omg", "ok", "idk", "imo", "pls", and "gonna" are no longer rewritten to a wrong dictionary neighbour ("lol"→"lot", "omg"→"ong", "ok"→"on"). The subtitle dictionary barely carries these, so the speller used to treat them as typos; they're now protected like everyday words. Works with the neural engine on or off.
- **No more mangled run-on splits.** A single misspelled word that's close to one real word ("tommorow", "definatly") is no longer chopped into two unrelated words ("tom moron", "de finally"). The splitter only fires for genuinely dropped spaces now.
- **Heavy typos autocorrect again.** Bad mistypes with no single-letter fix ("fkighy"→"flight", "stikk"→"still") commit on space once more, instead of only showing in the bar. The two-edit candidate is still gated by the confidence model, so an uncommon guess stays a tappable suggestion rather than forcing itself.
- **Custom words.** Words you add under Text → Suggestions now reach the keyboard without turning on learning, show up as you type them, stay off autocorrect's fix list, and sync while the keyboard is open.
- **Emoji tab bar scroll.** Dragging the category strip at the bottom no longer also flips emoji pages. Category swipes stay on the grid only.
- **Two-letter typos.** Short fat-finger mistakes like "mr" (me) and "ir" (is) now show a fix in the bar and autocorrect on space when the intended word is much more common. Common two-letter words like "be" and "is" are left alone.
- **Insertion typos without neural engine.** Extra-letter mistakes like "spaetial" now autocorrect to "spatial" on space even when Neural engine is off. A guard meant to block wrong shorter fixes ("tyaing"→"tying") was also holding back obvious deletion fixes with no same-length alternative.
- **Developer icon on device.** Debug builds on a real iPhone now swap to the purple grid icon after the splash finishes. The swap used to run too early and iOS cancelled it. Alternate icon sets no longer include clear style, which was dropping the default 1024 asset and showing the wireframe placeholder.
- **Keyboard sounds no longer spike in volume.** Key clicks could get noticeably loud until you switched apps, when the keyboard's audio session stayed active after the host app played sound or after a custom sound pack was selected without bundled samples. The keyboard now drops its audio session when it hides, when iOS interrupts audio, and before every system click.
- **Clipboard and notepad lists stay sharp at the top.** The scroll fade at the top of the clipboard and saved-notes panels showed even when you were already at the top. It now only appears once you scroll down and there is content above.
- **Spanish and other UI gaps.** Settings home cards (AI, Integrations), the search placeholder, Permissions setup steps, onboarding copy, Extras descriptions, and the Privacy page were missing from the string catalog, so they stayed in English on localized devices. Added 106 missing keys across all 10 app languages and a `make check-localization` guard so new UI copy can't ship untranslated. (`Localizable.xcstrings`, `Tools/AuditLocalization.py`)
- **Stopped silently mangling names and odd words.** A long word the dictionary
  doesn't know (a name, a handle, some slang or code) could get quietly swapped on
  space for a real word two edits away. That aggressive two-edit guess is now offered
  in the bar to tap instead of being forced, so what you typed stays put unless you
  pick the fix. Ordinary single-slip typos still autocorrect as before.
- **The previewed fix now matches what space actually types.** The little correction
  chip in the bar sometimes showed a different fix than the one space committed (most
  noticeably for dropped-space splits like "alot" to "a lot"). They're the same thing
  now, so what you see is what you get.
- **Typing stutter on corrections, take two.** The space-commit spell check had
  crept back onto the main thread. Two checks still ran there for every misspelled
  word: the "is this misspelled" probe (one checker call per active language) and
  the heavy edit-distance-2 search for long typos. Both now stay on the debounced
  pass that runs after typing settles, so the space key no longer waits on them.
  The checker also warms up once when the keyboard appears instead of on the first
  correction, which is where the longest hitch came from. As a side effect, a long
  word no longer gets silently swapped for a distant lookalike on the space commit;
  that two-edit guess is now offered in the bar to tap, not forced.
  (`SuggestionEngine.resolveCorrection`, `prewarmChecker`, `KeyboardViewController`)
- **Telex tone removal works on accented vowels.** Typing `z` to clear a tone (and
  re-typing a tone key to change one) had no effect once the vowel already carried a
  mark, because the syllable scanner didn't count accented vowels as vowels. It does
  now. (`VietnameseCompositor`)
- **Space no longer stalls on corrections.** The autocorrect committed on space
  ran the spell checker on the main thread for the just-typed word, and the
  cache meant to make that free only warmed after a 250ms pause, so a normal
  typist paid the full checker cost on every corrected word. The space commit
  now resolves the fix from the frequency lexicon (transposition and edit
  neighbours) instead of the checker; the checker's longer-tail fixes still
  arrive in the suggestion bar once typing settles.
  (`SuggestionEngine.resolveCorrection`, `correction(for:)`)
- **Metal themes show the settings panels.** The sidebar and pop-up sheets now
  paint the theme's background wash on Metal themes instead of going transparent;
  before, they only had a fill for Solid and Liquid Glass.
  (`RootView.sidebarPanel`, `ThemedSheet.panelBackground`)
- **Valid words stay put.** Correctly spelled words like "fixes" no longer get
  silently rewritten to a lookalike ("fixed") just because the subtitle corpus
  ranks them below top-tier frequency. Corpus-rare alone no longer opens the
  spelling path; real-word fixes need corpus-junk frequency or checker misspell.
  (`SuggestionEngine.resolveCorrection`)
- **"ill" → "I'll".** Apostrophe-less "ill" now expands to "I'll" (after "so ill",
  etc.) instead of drifting to "will". Blocked after adjective contexts like
  "feel ill". (`contractionCorrection`)
- **Lighter checker pass.** Edit-neighbour scans for the bar skip everything the
  checker already accepts as valid, so typing feels snappier again.
  (`typoBarCandidates`)
- **Instant autocorrect while typing.** Corrections now paint on every keystroke
  from the lexicon (contractions, edit neighbours) instead of waiting up to half
  a second for the checker quiet window. The checker pass still refines once
  typing settles. (`fastCompute`, `lexiconOnlyCorrection`)
- **Checker waits for a real pause.** Full spell-check enrichment now waits for a
  genuine typing pause (250ms) instead of the old 450ms, but no longer re-fires on
  every letter mid-flow. The spell checker is main-thread-bound, so a typist
  pausing 150-200ms between keys used to trigger it on each letter and stall;
  gating it on a true settle keeps it out of the typing rhythm. Cheap lexicon
  corrections still paint while you type. (`suggestionQuietWindow`)
- **No redundant heavy pass per keystroke.** The 80ms debounce tick no longer runs
  the heavy edit-neighbour correction (edit-1 forms across every active language)
  on top of the settled checker pass that already computes it. That duplicate
  main-thread pass after every key was the worst of the stall, especially with
  several languages active. (`debouncedSuggestionTick heavy:false`)
- **Contractions beat wild guesses.** "Whats" now becomes "What's", not "Chats".
  Contractions run with autocorrect on (not only smart punctuation), and the
  real-word typo path can't override a known contraction. (`contractionCorrection`)
- **Heavy typos get a second pass.** Edit-2 lexicon neighbours catch multi-slip
  words like "fkighy" → "flight" when edit-1 alone can't reach them.
  (`bridgedEditNeighbors`)
- **Fixed the typing freeze.** Pausing on a longer typo could lock the keyboard for
  a beat while it searched edit-2 corrections. That search built every two-edit
  spelling of the word (hundreds of thousands of strings for an 8-letter word, and
  again for each active language) on the main thread. It now uses the bounded
  bridge search on your primary language only, which reaches the same fixes without
  the freeze. (`editNeighbors2` dropped from `resolveCorrection`, `typoBarCandidates`;
  `bridgedEditNeighbors` primary-only)
- **No more stalling while typing.** The instant per-keystroke paint now does
  only cheap O(1) fixes (contractions, shortcuts, single-letter swaps). The
  edit-neighbour scans that drive typo correction enumerate edit-1 surface forms
  across every active language, which froze the keyboard mid-burst; they now ride
  the short debounce instead and fill the bar a moment after you pause. The real
  applied fix is still computed on space, so accuracy is unchanged.
  (`fastCompute heavyCorrection`, `lexiconOnlyCorrection`, `fastTypoBarCandidates`)
- **Rare corpus junk still corrects.** Words the subtitle lexicon barely knows
  (below the literal floor) go through the spelling path again, so typos like
  "wanja" → "wanna" aren't left alone just because the checker accepts them.
- **Quoted suggestion keeps your word.** Tapping the literal in quotes when a
  correction is pending now commits what you typed and adds a space, like the
  native QuickType bar. (`keepTypedWord`)
- **Corpus-absent typos autocorrect again.** When the spell checker accepts a word
  the frequency lexicon doesn't know (like "verdion" for "version"), Clink now still
  weighs edit-1 neighbours and can fix it on space. (`SuggestionEngine+Correction`)
- **Right-column keys stopped flickering on glass themes.** The key-photo mask used
  press-scaled bounds, so a neighbour's bloom slid its hole under p / l / m. The mask
  now tracks resting key frames only. (`KeyRestFrameKey`, `keyImageLayer`)

## Removed

- **Voice dictation.** The old warm-session microphone panel and its settings have been removed. Clink's writing assistance now centres on private on-device prediction, correction, translation, and Apple Intelligence tools.
- **The "Enable AI" toggle.** Folded into the single Apple Intelligence switch on Integrations (see Changed).
- **Voice dictation.** Removed dictation from the keyboard and the Clink app — the warm-session mic panel, recorder, bridge, and settings page are all gone.
- **No more swapping icons.** Clink used to switch to a blue or purple grid badge on beta and Xcode builds, and Extras had a picker to choose between them. That whole scheme is gone. There's one icon now, everywhere, and the picker has been removed from Extras.
