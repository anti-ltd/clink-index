NuKey themes import whole, Vietnamese Telex types the way Vietnamese typists expect, Shift stops recasing the word you just typed, and suggestions are back on iOS 17.

## New

- **More emoji in the one-handed gutter.** Recent emojis used to stop at six, leaving the bottom of the column empty. The count now defaults to Auto, which fills the height the keys take up (eight on a plain keyboard, ten or more with a number row or taller keys), and Keys → One-handed mode → Emojis sets a fixed 4 to 12 instead, with a Glyph size slider underneath for how big they're drawn. When you have fewer recents than slots, popular emoji fill the rest, so the column (and the in-app preview) is never blank. The side, extra-keys and emoji-count choices are now the same capsule rows as the rest of the app instead of dropdown menus.

## Fixed

- **Every theme can be shared as a QR code.** Themes with a photo backdrop, a key photo or Canvas prints used to stop at "Theme QR code unavailable". They now travel as a numbered set of codes that carries the images too, shrunk just enough to keep the set short, and arrive the way opening the theme's file would. Themes that already fit one code, or an existing numbered set, produce exactly the same codes as before. A keyboard preview that takes too long to capture no longer blocks the code either.
- **Suggestions are back on iOS 17.** The suggestion bar stayed empty on iOS 17: fields there don't say whether they are password fields, and the keyboard took that silence to mean they were, so it held back every suggestion the way it does for a password. A field that doesn't say is now treated as an ordinary one.
- **Emoticons after a word keep their space.** Punctuation spacing pulls a typed ";" or ":" against the word before it, which turned "hi ;)" into "hi;)". When the next key makes the mark into a face (";)", ":D", ";-;", ":/"), the space now comes back. Ordinary semicolons and colons still hug the word.
- **Chinese punctuation takes you back to Pinyin.** Return to letters waits for Space, and Chinese never puts a space after ， or 。, so every punctuation mark left you on the symbol page with an extra tap to get back. On the Chinese and Cantonese layouts a clause or sentence mark (， 。 ？ ！ ： ； 、 …) now returns to letters on its own, under the same setting. Digits and symbols like + or % stay on the page, and so does the dot or colon inside a number, so 3.14 and 10:30 still type in one go.
- **The expanded candidate grid scrolls from its side margins too.** They sat outside the scroll area, so a drag that started beside the first or last column went nowhere.
- **Vietnamese Telex types the way Vietnamese typists type.** Telex now reads the whole word instead of editing one letter at a time. A tone typed at the end lands on the right vowel ("aos" → áo, "thaays" → thấy, not aó or thâý), and a word only ever carries one. A third `a`, `e` or `o` cancels the circumflex ("aaa" → aa) instead of stacking another â, and a second `w` gives back "ow" or "uw" instead of flipping between o and ơ for good. "uow" makes ươ without a `w` after the u. Marks only go on something that can still be a Vietnamese syllable, so "tgaos" and "tpaays" stay as typed, and a word that stops being Vietnamese halfway through goes back to the keys you pressed ("ask", "hosting"), so most English doesn't need a keyboard switch. VNI and VIQR share the tone placement fix.
- **Shift stops recasing the word you already typed.** Tapping Shift arms the next character, but the suggestion bar and the word Space commits were both reading it as "capitalise the word being finished": type "this", reach for Shift for the word after it, hit Space, and the keyboard wrote "This". The chips had the same problem from the other side — they were cased when they were painted, so a tap on Shift left the bar promising one spelling and inserting another, either way round. A word whose own first letter is already on screen in lowercase now keeps the case you typed it with, the chips follow Shift while they are up so what you read is what lands, and a genuine sentence start still capitalises whether or not Shift is lit. Caps Lock is unchanged: it is a sustained choice, not a one-shot.
- **A saved capital can be typed back out again.** Clink remembers how you capitalise a word and restores that spelling on Space, which is what turns "swiftkey" into "SwiftKey". But a word it rewrites is deliberately not learned from — it was probably a typo — and that left the capital unable to lose: every later lowercase use was rewritten before it could count against it, so a word saved with a capital by mistake stayed capitalised for good. A rewrite that keeps every letter and changes only the case is a disagreement about capitalisation, not about the word, so the spelling you typed now keeps its say, and typing the word in lowercase a few times settles it.
- **NuKey themes come across whole.** A picture the theme printed on a single key — the face Venom HD puts on its backspace — was dropped, because their file names it one way on a key's own style and another way in the block of restyled keys, and only the second spelling was read. Where that picture does land, it now covers the key's own symbol the way theirs does, instead of wearing a delete cross on top of the face; a cut-out with transparency still leaves the symbol showing, which is what a cut-out is for. The keyboard-wide photo sat too high: NuKey hangs it from the bottom of the keys and Clink was centring it, which lifted the picture by half the band a wide photo spills past the top row. The shift key never showed the photo at all — it draws itself separately from every other key, and that path had no picture in it, so shift came out a plain coloured cap in the middle of a photographed board. And a key only a shade off the theme's letters — a spacebar or a 123 key a touch greyer than the rest — was painted flat instead of showing the photo, which left grey slabs in the middle of the board. And the photo now lands on the same keys it does in NuKey. Their skin is framed in a box a quarter taller than the keys (their toolbar strip sits on top of it) and shrinks about its middle, on a board flatter than Clink's; Clink was framing it against its own taller rows and hanging it from the bottom, so a cut-out like Hellforge's devil came out a row low with its tail across the spacebar. Themes already imported keep their old framing; import the file again to pick this up.
- **An imported photo keyboard keeps its sculpted keys.** A NuKey theme with moulded caps and a photo (Hellforge, Venom HD) came in as flat keys, because Clink's Mechanical cap could only wrap a photo down the whole of every wall, which bent each key's slice into a dish. The Mechanical cap can now wrap a keyboard photo just over the rim the way NuKey does, set by the theme's own wrap depth, so these imports stay sculpted with the picture lying flat on each face. A cut-out saved with Multiply blending tints the caps under it, as it does in NuKey. Themes NuKey renders as glass or Berry keycaps are unaffected.
- **A photo on your keys is framed to the keys.** A key photo was sized against the whole key block, which runs from the top of the suggestion bar down past the bottom row to wherever the keyboard ends — a good quarter taller than the keys themselves. So the picture was scaled against a box most of which no key can ever show: a wide photo came out enlarged and cropped at the sides instead of spanning the keyboard, and the same theme moved when the suggestion bar was switched on or off. It is now framed against the rows. A photo you positioned yourself may sit a little differently than before; open the theme in the builder and use **Adjust framing** under Keys → Extra to nudge it.
- **Learned words: corrections you can actually set.** Each pair under Corrections used to carry an On or Off badge that looked like a switch, could not be tapped, and named a state the keyboard does not really have: "On" only meant you had not undone it, never that Clink would make the change. The badge is now the control. Tap it and pick **Always**, **Still learning** or **Never**. Always is new — it makes the correction from the next word on, without waiting for Clink to be convinced, and it holds even for a pair Clink would normally leave alone because the word you typed is one it knows. Still learning says how far off it is ("2 of 3 picks"), so a pair that is doing nothing yet says so. Never is the old Off. A choice applies to every layout you have typed that pair on, survives clearing your learned words, and an undo on the keyboard still overrules it.
- **No more stray grid over the keys.** Hit Map's Show hitmap switch drew a warp grid and an arrow on every key, across every app you type in. It sat next to the Strength slider, so it got switched on while tuning tap correction and then looked like the keyboard had broken. It is a developer instrument and is no longer in shipping builds; if it was left on, the grid is gone. Tap correction itself is unchanged.
