Plugins, lighting effects you design yourself, and a 12-key Chinese keyboard.

## New

- **Plugins can offer looks.** Five new hooks hand the app things to pick, each shown under From plugins beside the built-in choices: `key_styles(state)` for key styles (a finish laid over the theme you're editing, from flat to low-profile mechanical), `popups(state)` for key popups, `animations(state)` for an entrance, a key press, a letter animation or the switch between letters, 123 and #+=, `backgrounds(state)` for animated backgrounds made of particle layers, and `layouts(state)` for layouts that install as ordinary custom layouts. A look is only numbers and words, so nothing runs per frame, and a picked look keeps working with the plugin switched off. Picking a built-in style puts the plugin's aside.
- **Plugins can change how typing feels and lands.** `haptics(state)` gives any key its own haptic (a heavier space bar, a crisp return), `hitboxes(state)` moves or resizes any key's target, and `on_touch(key, x, y, state)` says where on its key every tap landed, which is enough to build an adaptive hitbox. `suggestions(word, state)` puts words at the front of the bar while a word is typed, and `correct(word, fix, state)` decides what space commits: another word, the word as typed, or the keyboard's own fix.
- **Eight new official plugins.** One for each of those hooks: Low Profile, Bubble Popups, Motion Kit, Snowfall, Colemak-DH, Heavy Space, Adaptive Hitbox and Shorthand.
- **Build your own lighting.** Effects has a new Your effects section with a New effect tile. An effect is a stack of layers, each a pattern (solid, pulse, wave, gradient, twinkle, sweep, rain, flicker or checker) with its own speed, size, direction and brightness range, moving the brightness, the colour or both. Layers can add together, keep the brightest, or mask the ones above. Give it a palette of up to eight colours to travel through, or leave it empty and it follows your Color setting. The editor lights a live keyboard as you change things, and everything else about lighting (faces, letters, glow, bar, press flare, sleeping when idle) works with your effect the same as with the built-in styles. Pro, like the rest of Effects.
- **Plugins can offer lighting effects.** A plugin that defines `effects(state)` and returns `light_effect(...)` entries built from `light_layer(...)`s shows them under Effects > From plugins. A picked effect keeps working with the plugin switched off; while the plugin is running, the keyboard asks it again once a second, so an effect can follow the typing. The official repository adds Light Show, with Aurora and Tempo, a wave that keeps pace with your typing speed.
- **Plugin flicks.** Plugins get a new `on_swipe(direction, state)` hook for flicks that start on a letter key while swipe typing is off: `left`, `right`, `up`, `up_left` and `up_right`. Declare it as `on_swipe(key, direction, state)` instead and the flick's key comes with it, as a lowercase letter, so one hook can give each letter its own gesture without a plugin per key. A flick no plugin acts on stays an ordinary keystroke; one a plugin does act on takes its letter back first. Two new commands go with it: `press("space")` / `press("delete")` run the keyboard's own keys (so a space still autocorrects), and `pick_suggestion("left" | "center" | "right")` takes the chip under that part of the suggestion bar, scrolled bar included. The official Flick Gestures plugin uses them for Fleksy-style editing, with a switch each for delete, space and suggestions.
- **Plugin badges on the Gestures tiles.** A Gestures tile whose card a plugin manages now says "Plugin", the way the Spacebar tile already did.
- **A 12-key Chinese keyboard.** Layout → Arrangement → Chinese now offers **Chinese (Simplified Pinyin 12-Key)**: the 九宫格 grid Chinese phones put beside the letter board, twelve keys instead of twenty-six narrow ones, laid out the way Sogou and Gboard lay it out — the nine-grid itself as a square block of nine keys, and delete, 分词 and 。 in a column beside it. A key covers its whole letter group — ABC, DEF, GHI — so 你好 is five presses and every press means three or four letters at once. Which ones you meant is settled by the same word model, segmentation, sentence bigrams and learned phrases the QWERTY Pinyin keyboard already uses, so 你好 comes back as a phrase rather than a pile of homophones to assemble, and the composing text shows the Pinyin it worked out as you go. Everything else is the Pinyin keyboard you know: the candidate rail and its pages, committing part of a reading and carrying on, 分词 to force a syllable break, fuzzy Pinyin, Chinese punctuation, and phrases learned on one of the two keyboards offered by the other. Hold a key to pick one exact letter out of its group for a reading the grid won't guess. A Traditional twin ships with it. Longer phrases are worth reading off the rail: with three or four letters per press, the leading candidate is right about four times in five by character on our frozen 2,000-sentence set, against nine in ten on the letter board — and typing each syllable out in full is what the grid is for, since initials-only shortcuts (bjdx for 北京大学) carry almost no information once every press covers four letters.
- **Plugins.** A fourth tab, for scripts that run alongside the keyboard rather than inside a panel. A plugin hooks into typing (`on_open`, `on_key`, `on_word`, `on_close`), draws its settings with the same builders a panel uses, can put those controls on a native screen with `section("keys.spacebar", ...)`, and can take over a native control with `claim(...)`. It reads the live typing rate through `stats()` and a few settings through `setting(name)`, and can caption the space bar with `space_text(...)`. Plugins install from repositories (the official one is `anti-ltd/clink-plugins`, with WPM Spacebar to start), import as `.clinkplugin` files, and have an editor with a settings preview, buttons that fire each hook, and a console. Membership-gated like the other authoring tools.
- **A live typing rate.** Analytics only ever kept the best speed; the rolling window now stays on while plugins are enabled, whether or not Analytics is, so a plugin can show the rate as you type. Nothing about it is stored.
- **Scripts can tell which interpreter they are running on.** `import sys` now gives `sys.version`, the line `pymini 2.0.1`. PyMini is open source as a Swift package at github.com/anti-ltd/PyMini, and Clink's copy carries the same version number, so a script that runs in both can tell them apart.

## Fixed

- **A community Chinese pack's Pinyin table now reaches the keyboard.** Simplified Pinyin runs on a word model built into the app, and a `zh.cime` in the plain reading-then-choices format was quietly passed over for it, so a table published from your own repository changed nothing however it was written. Its rows now sit on top of the model: your choices for a reading lead, in your order, followed by the model's own, with phrases you've taught Clink still first. `pin yin`, `pin'yin` and `pinyin` are one reading, and a choice doesn't need to be in the pack's dictionary. Official packs are unaffected.
- **Animated backgrounds stay put instead of flickering.** The keyboard turns its effects down as it approaches the memory limit iOS gives it, and a busy board crosses that line often enough that the background was stepping between two levels of detail every few seconds: particles appearing and disappearing, the drift changing pace, the glow coming and going. It was most obvious on phones with less to spare, which is exactly where it was least welcome. Now it turns down once and holds there, and only climbs back to full detail once the keyboard has had room to spare for a while. Key lighting is steadier for the same reason. Nothing was ever wrong with the effects themselves, and on a phone with room to spare nothing changes.
- **A shared panel that defines `on_open` installs.** The source policy refused `open(` as a substring, which also matched the `on_open(` hook every panel is allowed to define. Fragments that name a call now have to stand as whole words.
